# App store metadata

This document holds the store listing copy for Harmony Frequency, with three
variants for A/B testing. It follows the voice guide and claim rules in
`docs/VOICE_TONE_AND_CLAIMS.md`.

Character limits used here:

- Apple title: 30
- Apple subtitle: 30
- Apple keyword field: 100
- Google Play title: 30
- Google Play short description: 80
- Google Play full description: 4000

## Positioning

The listing sells a need, not a frequency. A listener searching "help me
sleep" or "focus for study" should recognise themselves in the first line.

What the listing may say:

- frequency-based audio for sleep, calm, focus, meditation and intention
- a catalogue with plain-language names and a short note on each sound
- evidence labels: early research, traditional, popular practice
- free listening, then a 7-day trial and a paid plan

What the listing must not say:

- cure, heal, treat, repair, proven, guaranteed
- anxiety, depression, pain or any condition as something the app fixes
- wealth, income or financial outcomes
- medical or therapeutic advice

## Screenshot captions

Use these in order. Each caption names a need or a fact, not a technique.

1. What do you need right now?
2. Sleep, calm, focus or intention
3. Know what each sound is for
4. See the evidence behind each track
5. Start with 7 days free

## A/B test plan

Test one variable at a time and run each test for at least 14 days or until
the store reports a clear winner.

1. Subtitle: A against C. Sleep against calm. Measure store listing
   conversion and first-session start.
2. Screenshot one: "What do you need right now?" against the sleep
   category screen. Measure install-to-onboarding completion.
3. Short description: A against B. Measure installs from search.

Do not test price and copy at the same time. A price change moves revenue
even when copy does not.

## Keyword notes

- The keyword field is a list, not a sentence. Do not repeat words already
  in the title or subtitle.
- "ADHD" appears in variant B because people search it. The listing must not
  claim to treat ADHD. The word is a search term, not a promise.
- "Anxiety" appears in variant C for the same reason. The listing says the
  app supports calm, not that it treats anxiety.
- Do not use "healing", "therapy", "treatment", "cure", "binaural beats for
  anxiety" or "DNA" in store copy.
- Review the keyword field every quarter against the search terms report.

## Localisation notes

- Translate the need words, not the frequency names. "Deep sleep" should
  read as the local phrase for deep sleep.
- Keep Hz values and technique names unchanged.
- Recheck character limits after translation. German and French run longer.
- Recheck claim rules with a local reviewer. UK ASA and EU consumer rules
  treat health claims differently from US FTC guidance.
